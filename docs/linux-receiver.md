# Linux verifier and receiver

`LinuxVerifier` authenticates projected requests with the pinned envelope verifier and protected, request-bound time, assurance, identity and snapshot evidence. The owner grant root is P-256. `VerifyPublisherAttestation` authenticates the appointment and fixes the publisher algorithm: P-256 with `grant-revocation-1`, or Ed25519 with `grant-revocation-2`. The verified key must equal `PublisherKey`; the grant root, publisher, service root and agent revocation root are distinct. Other platform role separation remains a provisioning obligation.

Construct `LinuxReceiver` with explicit `PublisherKey`, `AgentRevocationRoot` and exactly those two enabled entries in the protected `Signers` map. Missing or aliased roles refuse admission. The receiver checks existing union and mark signers against those pins before any append. Publisher or agent-root rotation requires a newly provisioned state epoch. The verifier and receiver use the shared `mugrant.RequestTextAdmissible` rule.

The binding and epoch's `mu.journal` is the sole durable revocation authority, under the existing `mu.lock`. Kind 8 stores each signer's union; kind 7 raises only that signer's positive, whole-second, nonfuture mark. Every union append is synced before its mark. Authenticated future observations retain their entries without advancing a mark. Continuity compares only that signer's history: older timestamps and omissions refuse; equal complete snapshots and newer supersets may pass. Authenticated owner-root entries use the separate `owner-root` union, never a snapshot mark. Legacy global-only holds remain unattributed and continue to revoke without creating a signer completeness duty.

Each independently verified observation from the same invocation is retained even when its request refuses or the peer signer is unavailable or invalid. Adapter metadata is validated as a batch, including on a verifier refusal. The following faults return `ErrMUState` before any snapshot in that batch is appended, including a legitimate valid subset:

- A signer outside the pinned `Signers`, or a repeated signer.
- An invalid signer token: empty, more than 255 bytes, or any byte outside printable ASCII `0x21` through `0x7e` (spaces are excluded).
- An `issued_at` with a nonpositive Unix second or a fractional second.
- More than 64 entries in one snapshot.
- An invalid entry (`Kind` other than `record` or `key`, empty `ID`, or `ID` longer than 255 bytes), or a duplicate `(Kind, ID)` pair in one snapshot. The ID has no additional token or hash-format requirement here; the same ID with different kinds is distinct.
- More than 64 snapshots in a batch.

A corrected adapter's retry is admitted normally. Successful admission additionally requires a nonempty batch, every snapshot nonfuture and at most 60 seconds old, a publisher snapshot, and publisher entries matching the decision. The observation path on verifier refusal accepts valid old or future evidence; future evidence does not advance a mark.

The observation helper also returns `ErrMUState` when the next snapshot names a signer with no union and 64 signer unions already exist (including an owner-root union if present). Empty-entry snapshots do not create unions, but an absent signer still encounters this guard at capacity. Existing signers may extend their unions. This check occurs inside the append loop: a capacity refusal at the first snapshot leaves the journal unchanged, but does not promise to undo earlier successful snapshot appends. The receiver's two pinned snapshot roles normally prevent reaching this helper bound; direct journal tests cover it without widening the pins. The whole-batch metadata guarantee concerns that snapshot batch; protected owner-root evidence can already have been recorded before verification. A shared storage error stops the request and all further appends in that invocation; reopening must establish the durable prefix. A healthy peer has no independent storage fault domain. Admission and final identity, time, revocation and local scope checks precede reserve → intent → effect → outcome under the lock. The receiver's step-8 checks (audience, expiry, pause, widening, owner scope) take precedence over its step-9 revocation and rollback refusals, at first and final verification; the snapshots are recorded before either refusal. Grant-authority actions refuse with `mu-request-widens-authority` before the action-target check. UNKNOWN outcomes retain their holds and are never retried automatically.

No Linux sidecar is created or read as authority. Any file, directory or symlink named `linux-revocations.journal` refuses admission. There is no `LRA1` activation or migration reader. Retire any existing TEST/development state by explicitly provisioning a new private state directory and a new state epoch, reapplying authenticated owner-root revocations. Do not carry forward assumptions about old grants, outstanding UNKNOWN uses or old-epoch replay protection; the custodian must decide their policy. `RestoreLinuxReceiverState` enforces distinct epochs and reapplication of roots. This document performs no state operation. Framing and epochs do not detect a coherent rollback under the same identity and old configured epoch.

`TestLinuxInteropVectors` has an explicit served roster for all 89 canonical cases (109 steps). Mac authors are supported; there are no unsupported Mac-author cases. The seven invalid publisher configurations named in `linuxConstructionRefusals` are rejected during construction, as contract r8.1 permits. The rig records these refusals separately without fabricating platform step codes. All other steps independently exercise `LinuxVerifier` and the real `LinuxReceiver`, compare exact verdicts and effects, and reopen MU accounting and signer state. An unclassified new case fails the roster guard. The separate unknown-case fixture tests loss of that guard without altering the canonical corpus.

The sources and tests provide no live transport, protected clock implementation, A2 session provider, signing-service custody or effect provider. Identity-only revocations are immediate conservative holds. Native macOS, Keychain, service composition, hosted CI, cross-host operation, deployment and final landing require their external gates.
